Scope, corrections and contents

Included

  • Two-battery power distribution and low-voltage conversion.
  • Hardwired ON/OFF and DC contactor control chain.
  • Jetson, ESP32-S3, camera and WR850N network connections.
  • Traction, steering and mechanical-brake control interfaces.
  • AS5600 and AS5047P feedback wiring.
  • RP2040 Pico + RA-02 supplementary wireless stop.
  • GPIO allocation, connector schedule and pre-power checks.

Explicitly not installed

Raspberry Pi 4BRPLiDAR A1M8MPU6500 IMUsbrake encoder

The Jetson is the only main computer. The brake is presently open-loop STEP/DIR and has no confirmed AS5600 feedback.

Critical N16R8 correction

The installed ESP32-S3 has 16 MB flash and 8 MB embedded octal PSRAM. On this variant, GPIO33–37 are consumed by the flash/PSRAM interface.

GPIO35 and GPIO36 must not be wired to a brake AS5600.

This document marks those pins unavailable. If brake position feedback is added later, assign a new electrically valid interface and update firmware, pinout and validation records together.

01 / System architecture

Control, sensing and independent stop paths

High-level computer

The Jetson runs perception, cone-distance logic, the dashboard and mission decisions. It receives HP60C camera data over USB and exchanges commands/telemetry with the ESP32-S3 over USB serial at 115200 baud.

Vehicle control unit

The ESP32-S3 performs local actuator I/O: STEP/DIR, DAC command, BLD-750 control inputs, encoder sampling and optional LoRa packet reception. A Jetson command is not a substitute for the hardwired contactor chain.

Blue cones are stop objects. They are not part of corridor-centre steering. Braking logic and steering logic must remain separated so a blue cone cannot steer the kart into an unintended path.

No guessed fuse or cable values. Record battery short-circuit capability, continuous and peak driver current, buck input/output limits, contactor DC breaking rating, connector limits, cable length and ambient temperature first.
Common reference is deliberate. Bond signal COM/GND only where required by the interfaces. Never let a thin USB shield, encoder cable or sensor ground carry motor or brake return current.

Recommended physical order

BATTERY + → source fuse → service disconnect → contactor → branch fuse → driver/load

Place source protection as close to the battery as practical. The contactor and protection hardware must be DC-rated for the actual voltage and interrupt current; an AC rating is not interchangeable.

03 / Hardwired stop

Series ON/OFF button and DC contactor

Proposed series control circuit

12 V+ → branch fuse → maintained ON/OFF → normally-closed E-stop contact(s) → contactor coil +
contactor coil − → 12 V−

Opening the ON/OFF switch, pressing any normally-closed stop, breaking a series wire or losing the 12 V control supply drops the contactor. Its main poles interrupt the approved 48 V traction branch.

Hardwired path

  • No Jetson, ESP32, firmware, Wi-Fi or LoRa dependency.
  • Use maintained contact logic that does not restart automatically after power restoration.
  • Use a DC-rated coil and main contacts.
  • Fit flyback/TVS suppression if the contactor does not contain it.
  • Use an auxiliary contact for actual “traction energized” feedback if available.

Supplementary LoRa path

  • ESTOP,1 requests DAC 0 V, BLD-750 stop/brake and mechanical-brake application.
  • ESTOP,0 means remote switch released; it must not bypass a latched local fault.
  • Wireless stop is helpful, but packet reception is not proof of physical energy removal.
  • LoRa must remain supplementary until physically validated and safety-rated.
Beacon wiring. Power the 12 V beacon from its own fused branch. If it indicates “traction enabled,” drive it using a contactor auxiliary contact rather than only the ON command; that reports the physical contactor state more honestly.

What the drivers do — and why they are required

DeviceWhat it isWhy it existsController input
BM860High-current microstep driver for NEMA34 steering motorThe ESP32 cannot source motor phase current. The driver converts STEP/DIR commands into regulated winding currents.STEP, DIR, ENA through a compatible logic interface
TB6600Stepper driver for NEMA23 brake actuatorCreates controlled motor phase current and microsteps for the mechanical brake.STEP, DIR, ENA
BLD-75048 V BLDC motor controllerPerforms power commutation and accepts analog speed plus digital run/brake/direction controls.SV analog, EN, BRK, F/R
GP8630NI²C digital-to-analog converterTurns a software voltage command into the BLD-750 SV analog signal.I²C at 0x58
PN2222 stagesLow-side interface transistorsProvide the BLD-750-compatible input pull-down and protect/separate controller logic. They are not motor drivers.GPIO13/14/15 through base resistors

Before connecting either stepper driver

  1. Read the exact installed driver nameplate and terminal labels; “BM860” variants may differ.
  2. Measure whether PUL/DIR/ENA inputs are opto-isolated and determine common-anode versus common-cathode wiring.
  3. Verify 3.3 V logic compatibility or fit a proper transistor/line-driver interface.
  4. Set phase current from the motor nameplate, not from NEMA frame size.
  5. Set microstepping and record it in firmware. Current bench assumptions use 3200 pulses/revolution.
  6. Confirm motor direction with wheels raised before closed-loop control.
ENA is not an emergency disconnect. A logic enable can fail active and does not remove DC-bus energy. Use the hardwired contactor path for physical traction isolation.

Mechanical brake: TB6600 → NEMA23

ESP32TB6600
GPIO38PUL / STEP interface
GPIO39DIR interface
GPIO40ENA interface
19 V branchDriver DC supply

Current bench convention: applying the brake pushes the actuator pin inward/clockwise; arming/releasing retracts it in the opposite direction. Firmware currently treats the mechanism as open-loop.

Bench configuration records 30° application at 3200 pulses/revolution. Confirm travel, torque and hard-stop clearance physically.

No brake AS5600 is installed. Do not wire GPIO35/36. The brake must be physically released/homed before boot, and software cannot presently prove its position.

Required improvement for production

Add independent applied/released limit feedback or a valid position sensor on electrically available pins. Until then, missed steps, stalled motion or a disconnected coupler can remain invisible to software.

Straight-centre hold sequence

  1. Mechanically centre the wheels; capture stable AS5600 samples and save the median.
  2. Reject invalid magnet status, I²C faults and implausible jumps.
  3. Correct slowly only outside the configured deadband; stop pulses inside the band.
  4. Disable correction on E-stop or steering sensor fault.
  5. Revalidate centre after any magnet, coupler, chain or linkage change.

Routing rules

  • Route 48 V, BLDC phases and stepper phase cables away from I²C, SPI, USB and camera cables.
  • Cross power and sensor wiring at 90° where separation is impossible.
  • Twist differential/paired conductors and signal-with-return where appropriate.
  • Terminate shields deliberately; avoid uncontrolled ground loops.
  • Use keyed, locking connectors with strain relief and abrasion protection.
  • Do not splice high-current returns into controller/sensor ground wires.
  • Label both ends and record connector face views, pin numbers and wire colours.
  • Keep buck converters and motor drivers ventilated and mechanically secured.
